EN 10269

ENStandard

Steels and nickel alloys for fasteners with specified elevated and/or low temperature properties

← Back to topicSend a BOM or RFQ →
Key pointsBuying checksBasis

European material standard that defines steel grades, composition and mechanical properties of bar and wire for bolts and nuts, with properties specified at high and low temperatures. It is cited by flange bolting for pressure equipment (EN 1515).

Status: Current

Key points

  • It covers bar and wire of unalloyed steel, alloy steel, stainless steel and nickel alloys.
  • Steel grade examples: C35E, C45E, 20Mn5 (unalloyed steel), 25CrMo4, 42CrMo4, 40CrMoV4-6, 21CrMoV5-7 (alloy steel), X8Ni9 (1.5662), 1.4980, etc.
  • Besides room-temperature properties, it specifies service temperature properties such as 0.2 % proof strength at high temperature and impact values at low temperature.
  • ISO 898-1 and 898-2 also advise referring to EN 10269, etc. for material selection outside −50 °C to +150 °C.
  • "+QT" after the steel grade means the quenched and tempered condition.

Checks for BOMs and buying

  • Even for the same 42CrMo4, ISO 683-2 (formerly EN 10083-3, general quenched and tempered steel) and EN 10269 (for fasteners) certificates have different guaranteed items. For pressure equipment bolting, obtain a certificate that states EN 10269.
  • ASTM A193 grades and EN 10269 steel grades are separate standards, so do not write one as the same material as the other.
  • Do not set the upper service temperature limit by the steel grade table alone; check it against EN 1515 and the design code.

Equivalent and superseding standards

  • ASTM A193 · A320 · A453 US high-temperature and low-temperature bolting materials — correspondence is approximate
  • DIN 17240 Bolt and nut materials for high-temperature use — replaced by DIN EN 10269
