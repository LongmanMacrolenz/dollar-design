ISO 4042

ISOStandard

Fasteners — Electroplated coating systems

← Back to topicSend a BOM or RFQ →
Key pointsTableBuying checksBasis

Standard that defines the requirements for electroplating of fasteners (zinc, zinc-nickel, zinc-iron, cadmium) and the measures to prevent hydrogen embrittlement. For plated fasteners it takes precedence over general plating standards.

Status: Current

Key points

  • The main subjects are zinc, zinc-nickel and zinc-iron plating (+ conversion coating, sealer, topcoat, lubricant). Other platings such as tin, nickel and chromium are covered only for dimensional requirements.
  • Hydrogen embrittlement measures are divided by hardness: 360 HV or below no additional measure; over 360 up to 390 HV process validation and product testing or baking (manufacturer's choice); over 390 HV both process validation and baking.
  • Alkaline zinc-nickel (Ni 12–16 %) has a low hydrogen embrittlement risk, and baking may be omitted if there is test evidence and agreement.
  • For case-hardened screws (tapping screws, etc.), the measures are set by core hardness. Set screws that do not carry tension are exempt from baking.
  • The current edition drops the citations of ISO 2081 and ISO 19598 and is a self-contained standard for fasteners.

Summary of Tables 2–5 of the current edition of ISO 4042. Baking temperature and time are in the standard's annex.

Checks for BOMs and buying

  • For electroplated 10.9 and 12.9 products, check by certificate whether baking was done and the process validation records. Do not assume that baking alone completely eliminates hydrogen embrittlement.
  • For 12.9 socket head cap screws, consider black oxide or zinc flake (ISO 10683) first.
  • If a thick plating is specified, it may exceed the thread tolerance allowance and the nut may not go on.
  • Conversion coatings come with and without hexavalent chromium (Cr(VI)-free). The current edition was revised to reflect conversion coatings free of hexavalent chromium, so state the type at the time of order.
